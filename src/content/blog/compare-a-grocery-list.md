You already know the items. The open question is which nearby store is the simpler trip for the whole list, not which aisle has one cheaper can. WorthIt does that as a list, not as a pile of separate scans.

## What to type

Open the grocery list and add the staples by name. Milk, eggs, bread. Keep each line the thing you actually buy. A brand belongs on the line only when you need that exact item. Otherwise WorthIt prices a typical staple, not a specific box.

You can add up to 12 items. Quantity matters when you need more than one, so set it on the line instead of typing the item twice.

This check is not a photo. A shelf tag is still a scan. A list is typed, then compared in one pass.

Allow location when the phone asks. Nearby grocers come from where you are. If location stays off, WorthIt cannot find the stores for that trip. The compare also uses your signed-in WorthIt account, and the whole list counts as one analysis, not one per item.

## What you get back

WorthIt looks for grocers near you and prices the basket it can:

- A one-stop result, with an estimated total for the list at that store.
- The other nearby grocers it could price, so you can see the trip beside the winner.
- A note when splitting the list across stores might cost less. An extra stop is often not worth that gap. Read the estimate, then decide if the second store is actually on your way.

Those totals are estimates for typical staples at nearby grocers. They are not the live register price, and they are not a promise that every item is on the shelf. If you typed a brand, that line is the one WorthIt treats as specific.

## One trip or two

If one store can cover the list at a total you are fine with, go there. If the winner is far and a closer store is only a little higher, the closer trip can still be the better one. The screen is there so you do not have to open a different app for every item.

Run it before you leave, on the list you are about to shop. You are not trying to price the whole store.
