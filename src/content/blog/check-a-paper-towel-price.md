The big pack is easy to trust. It takes up half the shelf, the tag looks lower than the small pack you remember, and you are already in the store that sells paper towels in bulk. None of that tells you whether this pack costs less than the same count on Amazon, Walmart, or Target.

That is the decision. WorthIt is built for it, before the pack goes in the cart.

## Why a big pack feels cheaper than it is

A bulk tag compares a large count to a smaller one at the same store. It does not compare this pack to the same pack online. A warehouse or club price can also feel like a win, because a membership got you in the door. That fee is real. It is not a discount printed on the item.

The useful question is the tag in front of you: the digits for this pack, set against a typical range and online comps.

## What to photograph

Open WorthIt and snap the pack. Include the shelf tag when you can. You need the product and the price you would pay. If the roll count is on the front, keep it in the frame so the check is for the pack in your hand.

One photo starts a Save check. You do not pick Amazon first. WorthIt names the item, then shows:

- A deal signal, so you are not left comparing tabs in the aisle.
- A typical price range for that item.
- Online comps from Amazon, Walmart, and Target.

Those comps are estimates, not the live register price at the store you are standing in. Use them to decide, then confirm if you are about to spend real money.

If the tag was cut off in the photo, add the shelf price yourself so the comparison uses the number on the shelf.

## Buy the pack or leave it

If the shelf price sits under the typical range and under the online comps, buying the pack here is a reasonable call. If the tag is higher, put it back and order the same count.

Read the count before you trust a low tag. WorthIt prices the item in the photo. You still confirm the roll count matches the pack you mean to buy. A bigger pack is not automatically the better price. If you will not use the extra rolls, a lower tag on a larger count is still paper you have to store.

When the prices are close, the store can still be the better trip. You can take the pack home today. Shipping, tax, and a membership fee are yours to weigh. WorthIt does not subtract that fee, and it does not estimate a delivery date. If you need the rolls now and the tag is only a little above the comps, paying here can be the simpler call. If the tag sits well above those comps, walk.

Photograph the pack you were already unsure about. Leave the rest of the aisle. A typed grocery list is a different job: it compares nearby grocers for staples such as milk, eggs, and bread. A paper towel pack in your hand is a Save photo.
