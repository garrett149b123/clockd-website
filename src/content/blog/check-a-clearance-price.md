The clearance sticker is doing a small trick. It shows a lower number than the tag that used to sit on the shelf. It does not show whether Amazon, Walmart, or Target are cheaper for the same item today.

That gap is the decision. WorthIt is built for it, in the aisle, before the item goes in the cart.

## Why the sticker is a weak signal

A percent-off sticker compares this store to its own old price. The old price might have been high. The box might be last season, a dented carton, or a size you do not usually buy. None of that is printed in the discount.

Online prices move on their own. A clearance tag can still sit above a typical range. Some stickers are real deals. You cannot sort that by the color of the paper.

## What to photograph

Open WorthIt and snap the item. Include the clearance tag in the frame when you can. You need the product and the digits you would actually pay.

One photo starts a Save check. You do not pick Amazon first. WorthIt names the item, then shows:

- A deal signal, so you are not left decoding a spreadsheet in the aisle.
- A typical price range for that item.
- Online comps from Amazon, Walmart, and Target.

Those comps are estimates, not the live register price at the store you are standing in. Use them to decide, then confirm if you are about to spend real money.

If the clearance digits were cut off in the photo, add the shelf price yourself so the comparison still uses the number on the sticker.

## Buy or walk

If the clearance price sits under the typical range and under the online comps, it is a reasonable buy. If the sticker is higher, put it back. A loud markdown is not a reason to carry home an item you can get for less.

Look at the package before you trust a low number. A crushed box, an open seal, or a size you will not finish can erase the point of the discount. WorthIt compares the price. You still check the item in your hand.

## One sticker is enough

You are not trying to photograph the whole clearance rack. One item you were already unsure about is enough. Clearance gets expensive when every sticker feels like a deadline.

Skip this check when you only want to know which nearby grocer is cheaper for a whole list of staples. That is a typed grocery list, not a photo of one tag. For a single clearance item, Save is the check.
