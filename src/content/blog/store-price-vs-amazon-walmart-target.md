You are holding the item. The shelf tag looks fair. The open question is whether that same item is cheaper on Amazon, Walmart, or Target right now — or whether the aisle price is the one worth paying.

WorthIt answers that from one photo.

## Why “cheapest store” is the wrong question

Amazon is often strong online. Walmart and Target are strong on other days, and in other categories. Averages from a price study do not tell you what *this* SKU costs on *this* trip.

The useful check is local and specific: the digits on the shelf versus comps for the product in your hand.

## What to photograph

Open WorthIt and snap the item. Include the shelf tag when you can. You need the product and the price you are deciding on.

One photo starts a Save check. WorthIt names the item, then shows:

- A deal signal so you are not decoding a spreadsheet in the aisle
- A typical price range
- Online comps from Amazon, Walmart, and Target

Those comps are estimates, not the live register price at the store you are standing in. Use them to decide, then confirm if you are about to spend real money.

If the tag was cut off in the photo, add the shelf price yourself so the comparison still uses the number on the shelf.

## Buy or walk

If the shelf price sits under the typical range and under the online comps, buying in store can be the simpler trip. If the tag is higher, put it back — or finish the purchase online later.

Shipping, pickup, and membership fees still matter for the final total. WorthIt is the aisle check, not a checkout calculator for every fee.

## When this beats opening three apps

Standing in the aisle with Safari tabs for Amazon, Walmart, and Target is slow. One scan keeps the decision in one place. Use it on the item you were already unsure about — not every box on the endcap.

Download WorthIt free on iPhone and try it the next time a shelf tag makes you hesitate.
