A thrift tag can look cheap and still be a bad flip. The question is not only what you pay. It is what the item might sell for after fees, and whether that gap is worth the trip home.

WorthIt keeps that check on the same photo you already took.

## Start with the item in frame

Photograph the item the way you would for a shelf check. A clear product shot is enough. If there is a price tag, include it so you know the number you are paying.

The scan opens as Save first. Flip is one tap on the result. You do not photograph it again.

## What Flip shows

Flip is the resale view of that same scan:

- An estimated resale range.
- An estimated profit figure, so fees are not an afterthought.
- Marketplace context you can use before you list.

Treat those numbers as estimates. They are not a promise of a sale, and they are not live marketplace inventory. If the range is thin against the price in your hand, walk. If there is room, you still confirm the comps yourself before you buy a pile of them.

## When Flip is worth opening

Use it on things you might resell: thrift finds, clearance, or an item you already own and might list. Skip it when you only care whether the grocery tag is a deal. That is Save.

A short loop is enough: snap, open Flip, compare the tag in your hand to the estimated range, then buy or leave it on the shelf.

## Before you buy a stack

One decent estimate is a reason to look closer. It is not a reason to clear the shelf. Check the condition, the size, and whether you can actually ship or list it. If the estimated range only works when the item is perfect and the tag in your hand is already high, leave it.

Flip stays on the same scan as Save, so you can look at the shelf price and the resale view without a second photo.
