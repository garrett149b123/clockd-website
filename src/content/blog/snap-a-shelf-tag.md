You are standing in the aisle with a price tag in front of you. The number looks fine. It might also be a few dollars more than the same item online. WorthIt is built for that second.

## What to photograph

Open WorthIt and snap the item. Include the shelf tag in the frame when you can. The tag is the price you are actually deciding on. A blurry label or a price cut off at the edge makes the check weaker, so step back until both the product and the digits are readable.

You do not pick a mode first. One photo starts a Save check.

## What you get back

WorthIt names the item, then shows three things:

- A deal signal, so you are not left decoding a spreadsheet in the aisle.
- A typical price range for that item.
- Online comps from Amazon, Walmart, and Target.

Those comps are estimates, not the live register price at the store you are standing in. Use them to decide, then confirm if you are about to spend real money.

## Buy or walk

If the shelf price sits under the typical range and under the online comps, it is a reasonable buy. If the tag is higher, put it back. That is the whole point of the scan: you do not need a second app, a browser tab, and a guess.

If the tag was not in the photo, you can add the shelf price yourself so the comparison still uses the number on the shelf.

## After the first scan

Save is the job the first time you open the app. The same photo can also open Flip if you are checking resale, or you can type a grocery list later and compare nearby stores for one trip. You do not need those on the first scan.

A useful habit is one scan per trip, on the item you were already unsure about. You are not trying to photograph the whole aisle. One clear tag is enough to learn whether the price in front of you is the one you want to pay.
